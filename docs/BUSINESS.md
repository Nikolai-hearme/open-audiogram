# OpenAudiogram open-core business plan

OpenAudiogram's MIT-licensed core should remain genuinely useful on its own. The
free renderer, screening algorithm, reports, demo, documentation, and examples
create trust, distribution, integration demand, and a low-friction evaluation
path. Commercial products monetize operational convenience, validated hardware
workflows, hosting, support, and integration rather than withholding basic
interoperability.

## Product ladder

### Free MIT core

The repository is the funnel: dependency-free audiogram rendering, browser
screening primitives, printable reports, examples, and an active contributor
community. Success is measured through qualified adoption, clinic enquiries,
integration leads, and reusable ecosystem improvements—not artificial limits.

### Clinic Screening Kit

A one-time **$79–$249** package for small clinics, occupational-health teams,
schools, and community programs. It can include branded templates, deployment
checklists, consent and referral workflow templates, compatible-headphone
guidance, calibration record sheets, staff training material, and priority
setup support. Higher tiers can add multiple clinic brands and implementation
sessions.

### Hosted screening as a service

Managed screening links, secure result collection, clinic workspaces, audit
history, branded reports, exports, accessibility monitoring, and operational
support.

| Plan | Indicative price | Intended customer | Included value |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | Evaluators and very small pilots | Limited monthly screenings, basic report, community support |
| Clinic | $39–$99/month | Independent clinics and small programs | Custom branding, team access, result management, exports, email support |
| Enterprise | Custom annual contract | Networks, employers, public programs | SSO, regional hosting options, SLAs, security review, volume pricing, advanced audit controls |

Pricing should be tested against screening volume, staff time saved, referral
conversion, and compliance overhead. Avoid per-seat pricing where community
programs depend on rotating staff.

### Custom clinic and EHR integration

Implementation projects from **$2,000 to $20,000+**, depending on scope. Typical
work includes embedded workflows, FHIR/HL7 or vendor API integration, identity
and consent mapping, branded portals, data migration, security documentation,
custom analytics, validation support, and staff training. Recurring maintenance
and support should be quoted separately.

## Go-to-market

1. Publish useful educational examples and integration guides.
2. Invite audiologists and hearing-program operators into the roadmap.
3. Convert teams needing repeatable deployment into Screening Kit customers.
4. Convert programs needing storage, collaboration, governance, and uptime into
   hosted plans.
5. Offer scoped integration projects where workflow complexity creates clear
   economic value.

Partnerships with calibrated-headphone suppliers, tele-audiology providers,
occupational-health platforms, and EHR integrators can improve both distribution
and evidence quality. Any partnership claim should remain precise about the
validated combination of software, hardware, environment, and procedure.

## Clinical, regulatory, and trust boundary

The commercial model must not blur screening and diagnosis. Marketing a system
for diagnosis, clinical decision-making, treatment selection, or specific
medical claims can trigger medical-device regulation, quality-management,
post-market surveillance, and jurisdiction-specific registration. Even
screening products may attract regulatory, privacy, accessibility, consent,
cybersecurity, and professional-practice obligations.

Before making claims or launching in a market:

- obtain independent regulatory and clinical advice;
- define intended use, users, environment, contraindications, and referral path;
- validate the full device/headphone/browser workflow;
- document calibration, risk management, usability, accessibility, and change
  control;
- protect health data with appropriate contracts, retention, access, and
  incident-response controls.

Trust is the durable advantage: transparent limitations, clear evidence, and a
useful open core should remain product requirements.
